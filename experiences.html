01

Ouadane — The Forgotten Caravan City

If you want to feel like you have gone back 800 years, Ouadane is the place.

€950per person

One of Mauritania’s oldest cities and a UNESCO World Heritage Site: a historic ghost town in the heart of the Sahara, and a key stop on the caravan route between Morocco and West Sudan.

Founded in the 11th century, it was a centre for gold, salt and the book trade. Today it is abandoned, but its stone buildings, ancient mosques and old libraries still speak of its great past.

The silence here is heavy. You can hear the echo of your own footsteps.

Best for history lovers, photographers, and seekers of silence

4 days / 3 nights

Daily departures from Nouakchott

Highlights

  • The ancient mosque, collapsed stone houses and the ruins of the Portuguese fort
  • Libraries that once held 500-year-old manuscripts
  • Ouadane Valley and the old water well — the view of the city from above
  • Sunrise over the ruins, the best light of the whole trip
  • Two nights in a traditional desert camp, sleeping under a sky full of stars

What travellers experience

  1. 01

    Day 1 — Nouakchott → Atar

    7:00 AM pickup from your hotel or the airport. 4×4 across the desert to Atar with stops for tea and photographs in the dunes. Dinner and overnight in Atar.

  2. 02

    Day 2 — Atar → Ouadane

    8:00 AM departure, a 2.5-hour drive across the Adrar plateau of black stone mountains. Walking tour of the old city, desert lunch under an acacia, then Ouadane Valley and the old well. Night in a desert camp.

  3. 03

    Day 3 — Ouadane

    6:00 AM sunrise over the ruins. A deeper walk through the hidden alleys and old quarters. Afternoon with nomad families when they are nearby. Dinner and stargazing at camp.

  4. 04

    Day 4 — Ouadane → Atar → Nouakchott

    Breakfast and a last walk around the city, back to Atar for lunch and the market, then the long drive south. Arrival in Nouakchott around 10:00 PM.

What’s included

  • 4×4 transport Nouakchott – Ouadane – Nouakchott
  • Professional local guide and driver
  • 1 night in a hotel in Atar, 2 nights in a desert camp
  • All meals: breakfast, lunch and dinner
  • Tea and water throughout
  • Entrance fees

What to bring

Comfortable shoes for walking on stone, hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, camera, power bank.

02

Adrar Treasures

Terjit Oasis, Chinguetti, and two days on camelback in the Erg Amatlich.

€950per person

Terjit is a small village built around a natural oasis: palm trees, a canyon, and fresh spring water you can swim in after a day of dust.

Chinguetti is the seventh holy city of Islam and a UNESCO site, a key point on the trade routes that linked West Africa to the Mediterranean. Its libraries hold more than 3,000 books, some of them 1,400 years old.

Then the roads stop. Two days on camelback through the dunes of the Erg Amatlich with a Bedouin team — bread baked in the sand, tea with nomads, private tents under the Milky Way.

Best for travellers who want the desert without the crowds

  • Chinguetti’s ancient libraries and 1,000-year-old manuscripts

  • Learning to bake bread in the sand and the nomad tea ceremony
  • Ouadane ruins and the drive back through Atar

    Airport pickup and hotel.

  2. 02

    A five-hour 4×4 drive to the Adrar. Swimming in the spring, lunch under the palms, night in a traditional auberge by the oasis or camping.

  3. 03

    The Atar market for souvenirs, then the drive back to Nouakchott.

03

The Grand Crossing

Oasis, holy city, the Eye of the Sahara, and a night with the nomads.

€850per person

This is the route that shows the whole country in one journey. It starts in the capital and ends at the Atlantic, and in between it crosses every landscape Mauritania has.

Ouadane is the closest point to the Richat Structure — the Eye of the Sahara — and the night after visiting it is spent with nomadic families, who are around four percent of the population, learning how desert life actually works.

Further north, Ben Amera is the second largest monolith in the world, standing beside two mountains the Mauritanians call the male and the female.

Best for a first, complete crossing of the country

04

The Iron Ore Train

One of the longest and heaviest trains in the world, and you can ride on top of it.

€950per person

The Mauritania iron ore train is one of the longest and heaviest trains in the world, travelling over 700 kilometres from the mining town of Zouérat to the coastal port of Nouadhibou. It carries iron ore across the Sahara and takes about 15 to 20 hours.

Travellers ride it for adventure — often sitting on top of the open ore wagons, free of charge, or in a basic passenger car.

It is a harsh journey: dust, extreme temperatures and long hours. People do it for the thrill, the scenery, the photography, and to experience one of the world’s most rugged rail journeys.

Best for the thrill, the scenery and the photography
